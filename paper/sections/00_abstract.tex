\begin{abstract}
Off-policy evaluation for the current period must often rely on logs collected over many
earlier periods, with only a small sample from the period itself. Common remedies
(recency windows, decay, forecasting) treat every change alike and discard data that is
still informative. We argue that the useful question
is not how recent a period is but which mechanism changed: a change in the context
distribution or in the logging policy can be corrected by reweighting, while only a
change in the conditional reward biases a reweighted estimate. We propose \odrift{}, which
segments the history with a per-action residual test that is invariant to context and
logger drift, fits joint reward models across segments, reuses older segments with a
drift correction, and pools the segment estimates at minimum variance with a
random-effects interval. In a pre-registered evaluation with a one-shot test split, \odrift{}
outperforms a cross-domain estimator that groups periods by mean reward in all 15
reward-drift settings, and replacing its residual test by mean-reward grouping causes
spurious splits under context and logger drift; a second pre-registered test on fresh
environments, adding the time-feature estimator OPFV, passes every criterion again. On semi-synthetic Open Bandit data,
however, drift-corrected reuse is strongly biased; we report this failure and show
why validation on the small current-period sample cannot repair it. On real clicks with
natural drift (Open Bandit's A/B test, with Thompson sampling's own click rate as ground
truth), \odrift{} stays within 5\% of the best of 19 baselines.
\keywords{Off-policy evaluation \and Non-stationarity \and Concept drift \and Recommender systems}
\end{abstract}
