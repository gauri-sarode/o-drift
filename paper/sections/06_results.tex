\paragraph{Simulated regimes.}
\Cref{tab:main} compares \odrift{} with the best tuned baseline of each cell. Without reward drift
(S0--S2) it is within 7\% of it, the price of segment models that turn out to be unnecessary.
Under abrupt and mixed reward drift it wins (S3a: 7\%; S4: 14\% and 35\%, against a different
best baseline at each intensity), and the misspecification-3 cells of S3a, S4, S5 and S8 are won or
tied (0.80--0.99). It loses in four places: gradual drift (S3b) violates the piecewise assumption
and favours time-aware DR (1.28, 3.02); at the higher S3a intensity and in S5 the offset shrinkage
tuned on development data under-corrects large drift (S3a: 1.40; v1 without shrinkage would have
won, 6.5 vs.\ 7.0); and in S7 it rightly ignores support-deficient windows but loses to
min-variance pooling (1.52). The heterogeneity-inflated interval undercovers when bias remains
(S5, S3b: 0.22--0.63); the certified sample-split interval always covers but is 18 times wider.

\paragraph{Does the mechanism matter?}
Against tuned COPE, which groups windows by mean reward, \odrift{} has lower RMSE in all 15
reward-drift cells (0.19--0.93, intervals excluding zero) and ties it in S0--S2; COPE is better
only with faulty propensities or lost support (S6, S7: 1.07, 1.30). The grouping ablation replaces
only the monitor statistic by the mean reward. The residual monitor never splits a history with
stable reward (S0--S2, S6--S7), as \cref{prop:invariance} predicts; the mean-reward statistic splits
22--61\% of S1 histories (context drift), 52\% of S2 histories (logger switches) and every S7
history, costing 7--13\% RMSE in S1 and S2. Under reward drift the blind grouping is never better
and up to $2.35\times$ worse, mainly because it misses abrupt changes (S3a, lower intensity: 29\%
of episodes detected, against 100\%).

\paragraph{Replication and OPFV.}
The v6 registration reran the frozen method on four fresh environments (right columns of
\cref{tab:main}); all 14 registered criteria pass again, including R1--R2 on the
misspecification-3 cells and the new check against OPFV (N3). The pattern replicates: within 4\%
without reward drift; wins in S3a and S4 at the lower intensity (0.84, 0.88), S5 (0.93, 0.97), S8
and the misspecification-3 cells of S3a, S5 and S8; losses under gradual drift (S3b, intensity 1:
2.49), in S3a at intensity 1 (1.21) and in S7 (1.41); COPE is beaten in all 15 reward-drift cells.
Two wins did not replicate: S4 at intensity 1 (0.65 in the first test) is a tie with transport
balanced decay (1.01), and S4 at misspecification 3 is a loss (1.16). OPFV has 1.3--6.7 times the
RMSE of \odrift{} in every cell. Its selection rule measures each feature's bias against the
finest-feature estimate, which rests on the 1{,}000-record anchor; that noise swamps the bias term,
so the choice is nearly random (the all-windows feature is chosen in 19\% of S0 episodes). With the
coarsest feature fixed, OPFV is time-aware DR, which is among the baselines.

\paragraph{Open Bandit: where drift-corrected reuse fails.}
\Cref{tab:obd} shows the pre-registered robustness test on Open Bandit. Without reward drift
\odrift{} matches the best baseline, but under reward drift it is 3.1--7.7 times worse, and
almost all of its error is bias ($+14$ to $+68\times10^{-3}$). The monitor is not the cause: it
finds the change in every S3a episode, and monitor-select (same segmentation, no reuse) is
best in S3a and within $1.4\times$ of the best in S4. The base
reward is quadratic, the reward models linear (the basis rule chose linear features in every
episode), and the drift three times the standard deviation of the base logit, so the offsets
$\delta_j$ absorb model error as well as drift and the corrected segments carry a bias the
anchor cannot observe. Shrinkage makes this worse (S3a, intensity 1: 36.4 vs.\ 10.0 without shrinkage). Robustness criteria R1--R2
therefore fail; the other ten pre-registered criteria (C1--C5, N1--N2) pass.

\paragraph{Real rewards and natural drift.}
On the real-reward Open Bandit test (\cref{tab:real}) the click rate of the target policy moves
between blocks (from 4.1 to $8.0\times10^{-3}$), but these changes are smaller than the error
of any estimator at these sample sizes. As registered in advance, the monitor almost never
splits (0.7\% of episodes) and pooling everything is close to optimal: tuned COPE, whose
cluster spans all windows, is best overall. \odrift{} is within the registered margin
(ratio 1.05, task-bootstrap interval 1.01--1.09; criterion E1 passed), is the most accurate
method on the 80-item campaign (0.97, 0.94--0.99) and is 10\% behind on Men. The
offset shrinkage of v3 matters here: without it, \odrift{} has RMSE $3.56\times10^{-3}$ on
Men. OPFV, whose rule picks fine time features, has four times the error of pooling. The interval
of \odrift{} covers in 75\% of episodes. In 4 of 420
episodes the anchor contained no click, its plug-in variance is zero, and the pooling of \odrift{}
and the transport baselines is undefined; these episodes are excluded above. Post hoc, flooring
that variance at one pseudo-click gives estimates in all 420 episodes with the same ratio (1.05).

\paragraph{Can reuse be validated on the data at hand? (post-hoc).}
On development data (52 matched episodes per cell), a gate (v4) that drops a segment differing
from the reference at the 5\% level removes the bias but loses in S4 ($1.32\times$; $1.61\times$ at
misspecification 3), and a rolling-origin back-test (v5) that uses the newest window as a
pseudo-target rejects reuse in 31--54\% of episodes without reward drift and loses in six of nine
cells (up to $2.1\times$). Neither was tested. Both fail as \cref{sec:setting} predicts: anchor DR
has RMSE $42\times10^{-3}$, the decisive reuse bias is about $10\times10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{Anchor size and cost (post-hoc).}
With anchors of 100, 1{,}000 and 10{,}000 records, anchor-only DR follows $1/\sqrt{n_T}$ (RMSE 153, 42,
$12\times10^{-3}$) while \odrift{} hardly moves (S4: 14.2, 14.1, 11.1; no anchor 68.5), but under
gradual or seasonal drift (S3b, S5) the 10{,}000-record anchor alone wins (11.9 vs.\ 23.5, 29.3):
reuse bias does not shrink with $n_T$. On one core, \odrift{} takes 1.0\,s per episode and policy in
the simulator and 1.5\,s on Open Bandit, against 0.6 and 0.3\,s for COPE and 0.5 and 0.1\,s for
pooled DR; the monitor and the joint segment fits are the extra cost.
