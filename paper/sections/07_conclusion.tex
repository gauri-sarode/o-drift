\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

For OPE under drift, which mechanism changed a window matters more than its age, and a drift
decision is only as good as the data that can observe it. A residual test on the conditional
reward does not mistake context or logger drift for reward drift, which in our pre-registered
evaluation separated \odrift{} from mean-reward grouping in every reward-drift setting.

The limitations are as important as the result. First, drift-corrected reuse depends on the
reward-drift difference lying in the model class (A4); on semi-synthetic Open Bandit data,
with a misspecified model and large drift, it was heavily biased, and the estimator without
reuse was the better choice. Second, checks on the anchor or a held-out window could not repair this: at per-period
sample sizes the reuse bias is not observable, an information limit that binds any method
validating reuse on the current period alone. Third, gradual drift
violates the piecewise assumption and favours methods that model a trend. Fourth, our
heterogeneity-adjusted interval undercovers when bias remains, and the certified alternative
is wide. Finally, on real clicks the natural drift was below what the anchor can observe,
so that test shows \odrift{} is safe when there is little to correct (within 5\% of the best
baseline), not that it helps; logs with larger natural reward drift remain open.

Two directions follow: reward models flexible enough to make (A4) plausible, so that reuse is
safe when attempted, and reporting for each estimate how much unobservable drift would change
a deployment decision.
