\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Off-policy evaluation.}
Inverse propensity scoring~\cite{horvitz1952generalization} and the doubly robust (DR)
estimator~\cite{dudik2011doubly} are the standard tools for contextual bandit OPE; we
cross-fit the reward models as in double machine learning~\cite{chernozhukov2018double}.
Weighting by the mixture of several logging policies (balanced or multiple importance
weighting) reduces variance~\cite{agarwal2017effective,kallus2021optimal}, and density-ratio
transport handles covariate shift~\cite{uehara2020offpolicy}. \odrift{} uses all three; it adds
the decision of which historical data the reweighted estimate may use.

\paragraph{Non-stationary OPE.}
Jagerman et al.~\cite{jagerman2019when} study sliding-window and exponentially decayed
estimators for non-stationary bandits. Thomas et al.~\cite{thomas2017predictive} and
Chandak et al.~\cite{chandak2020towards,chandak2022offpolicy} forecast the future value of a policy from
its past per-period estimates, and Shimizu et al.~\cite{shimizu2025offpolicy} estimate
future values by exploiting time features shared between past and future. Liu et
al.~\cite{liu2023asymptotically} reuse a reward model fitted on older data inside a DR
estimator on the new data. These methods weight data by time or by a model of the trend
in the value; none distinguishes the mechanism behind a change.

\paragraph{Cross-domain OPE.}
COPE~\cite{natsubori2025cross} treats other domains as sources for a target domain,
clusters domains by the similarity of their mean rewards and combines multiple importance
weighting inside the target cluster with a decomposed reward model. With time
windows as domains it is the closest existing estimator to ours, but its clustering statistic,
the mean reward, mixes all three mechanisms; we include it as a tuned baseline and as a
grouping ablation.

\paragraph{Estimator selection and drift detection.}
MAGIC~\cite{thomas2016data}, SLOPE~\cite{su2020adaptive} and adaptive weighting~\cite{zhan2021offpolicy}
choose among or blend estimators of increasing bias and decreasing variance (baselines here,
over nested recency windows); drift detection on streams is surveyed in~\cite{gama2014survey}. Our monitor is a heterogeneity test~\cite{cochran1954combination}
on per-action residuals and our interval the DerSimonian--Laird interval~\cite{dersimonian1986meta};
the novelty is the statistic tested, not the tests. Selecting segments by a preliminary test is a
pre-test estimator~\cite{bancroft1944biases}, with its known risk near the threshold.
