\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Off-policy evaluation.}
Inverse propensity scoring~\cite{horvitz1952generalization} and the doubly robust (DR)
estimator~\cite{dudik2011doubly} are the standard tools for contextual bandit OPE; we
cross-fit the reward models as in double machine learning~\cite{chernozhukov2018double}.
When the data come from several loggers, weighting each record by the mixture of the
logging policies (balanced or multiple importance weighting) reduces variance and stays
unbiased~\cite{agarwal2017effective,kallus2021optimal}. Covariate shift between the
logging and target populations is handled by density-ratio transport~\cite{uehara2020offpolicy}.
O-DRIFT uses all three ingredients; what it adds is the decision of which historical
data the reweighted estimate may use.

\paragraph{Non-stationary OPE.}
Jagerman et al.~\cite{jagerman2019when} study sliding-window and exponentially decayed
estimators for non-stationary bandits. Thomas et al.~\cite{thomas2017predictive} and
Chandak et al.~\cite{chandak2020towards,chandak2022offpolicy} forecast the future value of a policy from
its past per-period estimates, and Shimizu et al.~\cite{shimizu2025offpolicy} estimate
future values by exploiting time features shared between past and future. Liu et
al.~\cite{liu2023asymptotically} reuse a reward model fitted on older data inside a DR
estimator on the new data. These methods weight data by time or by a model of the trend
in the value; none distinguishes the mechanism behind a change.

\paragraph{Cross-domain OPE.}
COPE~\cite{natsubori2025cross} treats other domains as sources for a target domain,
clusters domains by the similarity of their mean rewards and combines multiple importance
weighting inside the target cluster with a decomposed reward model. Treating time
windows as domains makes COPE the closest existing estimator to ours. Its clustering
statistic, the mean reward, mixes all three mechanisms; our monitor isolates the one
that biases reweighted estimates. We include COPE as a tuned baseline and as a grouping
ablation.

\paragraph{Estimator selection and drift detection.}
MAGIC~\cite{thomas2016data}, SLOPE~\cite{su2020adaptive} and adaptive weighting~\cite{zhan2021offpolicy}
choose among or blend estimators of increasing bias and decreasing variance; we use the
first two as baselines over nested recency windows. Drift detection on data streams is
surveyed by Gama et al.~\cite{gama2014survey}. Our monitor is a heterogeneity test in the
sense of Cochran~\cite{cochran1954combination} applied to per-action residuals, and our
interval is the DerSimonian--Laird random-effects interval~\cite{dersimonian1986meta}; we
claim novelty for the statistic being tested, not for the tests. Choosing segments by a
preliminary test is a pre-test estimator~\cite{bancroft1944biases}, and it shares the
known risk of such estimators near the detection threshold.
