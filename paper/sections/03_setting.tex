\section{Problem Setting}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Data.}
Time is divided into windows $k=1,\dots,K$ followed by a target period $T$. Window $k$
contributes logged records $\mathcal{D}_k=\{(x_i,a_i,r_i,\mu_k(a_i\mid x_i))\}_{i=1}^{n_k}$ with
contexts $x\sim P_k$, actions $a\sim\mu_k(\cdot\mid x)$ from the logger deployed in
window $k$, and binary rewards with conditional mean $q_k(x,a)$. From the target period we
have a small anchor $\mathcal{D}_T$ of $n_T\ll\sum_k n_k$ logged records and a sample
$\{x^*_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ of unlabelled target contexts. The estimand for a target policy $\pi$ is
\begin{equation}
  V_T(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_T}\Big[\sum_a \pi(a\mid x)\,q_T(x,a)\Big].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Three drift mechanisms.}
A window can differ from the target in $P_k$, in $\mu_k$ or in $q_k$. With logged
propensities and a density ratio $w_k(x)=P_T(x)/P_k(x)$, the reweighted DR score of a record
in window $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dr}
  \Gamma_k(x,a,r)=\mathbb{E}_{x'\sim P_T}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{a'}\pi(a'\mid x')\,\hat q(x',a')\Big]
  + w_k(x)\,\frac{\pi(a\mid x)}{\mu_k(a\mid x)}\,\big(r-\hat q(x,a)\big),
\end{equation}
has expectation $\mathbb{E}_{P_T}[\sum_a\pi(a\mid x)q_k(x,a)]$ for any $\hat q$. It
equals $V_T(\pi)$ exactly when $q_k=q_T$ on the support of $\pi$ under $P_T$. Context and
logger drift therefore cost variance but not bias; reward drift costs bias that no
reweighting removes. The transfer bias of window $k$ is
$b_k=\mathbb{E}_{P_T}[\sum_a\pi(a\mid x)(q_k-q_T)(x,a)]$.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
(A1) Logged propensities are the ones used at event time.
(A2) Overlap: $\pi(a\mid x)>0$ implies $\mu_k(a\mid x)>0$ for the windows used, and the
context ratio $w_k$ is bounded.
(A3) Reward drift is piecewise: the windows split into contiguous segments with a
common $q$ within each segment.
(A4) For drift-corrected reuse, the difference between segment rewards lies in the
reward-model class (on the logit scale).
Our regimes violate each assumption in turn (S6 breaks A1, S7 A2, S3b and S5 A3, S8 and
the misspecified cells A4) so that we can see where the method fails.

\paragraph{Observability of reward drift.}
Without an anchor, $b_k$ is not identified from history: the logs constrain only
$q_1,\dots,q_K$, any $q_T$ is consistent with them, and no method can certify an estimate. With
an anchor, $b_k$ is identified, but only up to the noise of the anchor estimate, whose
standard deviation is of order $\sigma/\sqrt{n_T}$. Biases below this level cannot be
detected from the anchor, however the test is built: a level-$\alpha$ test with power
$1-\beta$ needs $|b_k|\gtrsim(z_{\alpha/2}+z_\beta)\,\sigma/\sqrt{n_T}$. We call drift
\emph{observable} when it exceeds this threshold for the sample that must reveal it. Drift
between two historical windows is judged on $n_k$ records and is observable down to small
sizes; drift between the history and the target is judged on the $n_T$ anchor records and is
observable only when large. This asymmetry shapes the design in \cref{sec:method} and
governs the failure we report in \cref{sec:results}.
