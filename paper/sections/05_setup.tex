\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Simulated regimes.}
Each environment has $d=5$ Gaussian contexts, 10 actions and per-action logistic rewards
with a linear part and a nonlinear part of weight $0.5$ (``misspecification 0.5'') or $3.0$.
The level $3.0$ was fixed by matching the nonlinear share of logit variance in Open Bandit
($0.43$ vs.\ $0.41$) before any result at that level was seen. The history has $K=12$ windows
of 5{,}000 records; the anchor has 1{,}000 records and there are 10{,}000 target
contexts. Ground truth is exact over actions and Monte Carlo over $10^6$ contexts.
The regimes isolate one mechanism at a time:
S0 stationary;
S1 context drift growing with age;
S2 logger switches A$\to$B$\to$C;
S3a abrupt reward drift in the older half;
S3b gradual reward drift towards the target;
S4 mixed (oldest third reward-drifted, middle third context-shifted, newest third poorly
explored);
S5 seasonal context and reward drift;
S6 wrong logged propensities (violates A1);
S7 recent windows that never play three actions (violates A2);
S8 reward drift along an out-of-class interaction, S8b the same without an in-class component
(violate A4). Intensities are given with each regime in \cref{tab:main}. Four candidate
policies of increasing noise around the optimal policy are evaluated in every episode.

\paragraph{Open Bandit semi-synthetic data.}
We use the uniformly random logging data of the Men campaign of the Open Bandit
Dataset~\cite{saito2021open} (452{,}949 records, 34 items). Real user features (8 principal
components), in real time order, form 13 equal blocks (12 history windows and the target). The base reward is a
per-action logistic model with linear and quadratic terms fitted to the real clicks and
shifted to a mean click rate of $0.05$; actions, propensities, rewards and the injected
logger and reward drift are synthetic. Because the generating model is quadratic, the
methods' reward models are misspecified. These data test robustness, not natural drift.

\paragraph{Open Bandit with real rewards.}
To test natural drift we use every campaign of the dataset (Men, Women, All; 34, 46 and 80
items) with nothing synthetic. The uniformly random log (0.45--1.37 million impressions over
seven days) is cut into 19 time blocks of about 8.8 hours. A task takes block $b\in\{12,\dots,18\}$
as the target and the 12 blocks before it as history (5{,}000 records per window, an anchor of
1{,}000 and 10{,}000 target contexts drawn from block $b$). The target policy is the Bernoulli
Thompson Sampling (BTS) policy that ran in the same A/B test, represented by its item
frequencies per position in block $b$; the ground truth is its on-policy click rate in that
block (0.2--0.7 million impressions, standard error $\le0.2\times10^{-3}$). Contexts are 8
principal components of the user features and the position. Interactions between items shown
together are ignored, as in~\cite{saito2021open}. 21 tasks $\times$ 20 episodes.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
All share the reward models and basis rule: pooled, anchor-only, recent-$k$ and decayed
DR~\cite{jagerman2019when}; minimum-variance pooling; transport DR and its recent, decayed and
balanced-decayed variants; regression-assisted DR~\cite{liu2023asymptotically};
SLOPE~\cite{su2020adaptive} and MAGIC~\cite{thomas2016data} over nested windows;
forecasting~\cite{thomas2017predictive}; time-aware DR (polynomial time trend in the reward
model); balanced DR~\cite{agarwal2017effective}; an anchor-validated window selector;
COPE~\cite{natsubori2025cross} (windows as domains, anchor as target, cluster size tuned); OPFV~\cite{shimizu2025offpolicy},
which importance-weights the records sharing the target's time feature and chooses that feature per
episode from recency and periodic candidates (including the true seasonal period) by its
bias--variance rule; and monitor-select, DR on the newest monitor segment plus the anchor without
reuse. COPE and OPFV follow the estimators of the original papers (their Eqs.~9--10 and 4, 7) with our
shared reward-model class.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The protocol (\texttt{configs/protocol.yaml} in the code) was fixed before tuning. Every
method family, baselines included, is tuned on a development split (four environments,
200 episodes per cell) by minimising the mean over cells of log RMSE. Test splits use
unseen environments (300 episodes per cell; 200 for Open Bandit) and are run once per
frozen configuration. The method reported here is the third registered version; the first failed on Open Bandit, which
led to the misspecification-3 cells, robustness criteria, per-episode basis, offset shrinkage, COPE
and the grouping ablation. A later registration (v6) kept the method frozen and added OPFV, a
replication on fresh test environments and the real-reward test, with criteria fixed before running.
Twelve criteria were pre-registered: five on accuracy, coverage, support and abstention (C1--C5),
two on robustness in the misspecification-3 and Open Bandit cells (R1: no ratio above 1.5;
R2: ratio at most 1 in at least half of them), two against COPE (N1) and three on the grouping
ablation (N2); their exact wording is in the protocol file.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
RMSE ($\times10^{-3}$); its ratio to the best tuned baseline with a paired bootstrap 95\%
interval of the difference (10{,}000 resamples); interval coverage; and the share of episodes split.
